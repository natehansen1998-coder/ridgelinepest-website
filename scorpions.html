Finding Scorpions in Your Home?

If you've spotted a scorpion in the bathtub, the garage, or worse, a bedroom, you're dealing with one of Washington County's signature pests. Our desert climate, rocky landscaping, and block-wall construction give scorpions everything they need, and new construction constantly stirs them out of the ground and toward nearby homes.

At Ridgeline Pest Control, we treat scorpions differently than general pests, because they are different: scorpions can go months without food, resist many standard sprays, and squeeze through gaps as thin as a credit card. Effective control means targeting them directly, eliminating the insects they feed on, and sealing the routes they use to get inside.

Scorpions in the St. George Area

Southern Utah is home to several scorpion species. Knowing which one you're seeing matters, because one of them is medically significant:

Arizona Bark Scorpion

Small, slender, tan scorpion and the most venomous in North America. An excellent climber found in parts of Washington County, and the one to take seriously.

Desert Hairy Scorpion

The largest scorpion in the U.S. at up to 6 inches. Intimidating, but its sting is usually comparable to a bee sting.

Stripe-Tailed Scorpion

Stocky, medium-sized scorpion with dark stripes on the tail. Common under rocks and landscaping throughout the area.

Not Sure Which You Have?

Bark scorpions climb walls and hide in ceilings; ground scorpions stay low. Send us a photo or let us identify it during your inspection.

Bark Scorpion Sting Safety

Bark scorpion stings are painful and can be medically significant, especially for young children, the elderly, and pets. If a child is stung or anyone develops symptoms beyond localized pain (numbness, difficulty breathing, muscle twitching), seek medical attention right away. The Utah Poison Control line is 1-800-222-1222. If you're seeing scorpions inside your home, don't wait for a sting to act.

Why Do I Have Scorpions?

Scorpions end up in Washington County homes for predictable reasons. Understanding them is the key to long-term control:

  • Food supply - Crickets, roaches, and other insects around your home are a scorpion buffet. Control the prey and the predators leave.
  • Water in the desert - Irrigation, drips, and pool areas concentrate insects and the scorpions that hunt them.
  • Block walls and rock landscaping - The signature look of St. George yards is also perfect scorpion harborage.
  • New construction - Grading and excavation displace scorpions into surrounding neighborhoods.
  • Entry gaps - Door thresholds, weep screeds, plumbing penetrations, and window screens with tears.

What to Expect After Treatment

Scorpion control is a process. Here's the honest timeline:

  • Immediate - Direct treatment of active harborage areas; sightings often drop within days
  • Weeks 1-3 - You may still see the occasional scorpion as displaced ones contact treated zones
  • Ongoing - As the prey-insect population falls, scorpion pressure drops with it
  • Long term - Recurring service plus sealing keeps the population from rebuilding

Preventing Scorpion Problems

Between professional treatments, these habits make your home a much harder target:

  • Shake out shoes, towels, and clothing left on the floor or outside
  • Install door sweeps and repair torn window screens
  • Seal cracks in stucco, foundations, and around pipe penetrations
  • Move woodpiles, rocks, and stored items away from the house
  • Switch outdoor lights to yellow "bug lights" that attract fewer prey insects
  • Fix outdoor leaks and overwatering: moisture attracts the insects scorpions eat
  • Check bedding and cribs in rooms where scorpions have been seen
