Why Pest Problems Change With the Seasons

Washington County sits on the edge of the Mojave Desert, and that changes everything about pest control here. Winters are mild, summers are extreme, and the July-September monsoon brings sudden moisture to a very dry landscape. Instead of a long winter break, St. George pests stay active most of the year — what changes season to season is which pests are pressing hardest and where they're trying to get in.

Understanding these desert patterns is key to effective pest control. At Ridgeline Pest Control, we adjust our treatment strategies throughout the year to target the specific pests that are most active in each season, giving you continuous protection.

🌸 Spring Pest Control (February - May)

Spring comes early to St. George. By February the desert is already warming, and pest activity ramps up fast — ant colonies expand, scorpions come out of their winter slowdown, and wasp queens start new nests. Early intervention now prevents summer infestations.

Common Spring Pests

Ants

Colonies expand rapidly and forage aggressively as soil warms

Scorpions

Emerge from winter harborage and begin hunting at night

Spiders

Black widows become active and start building webs around homes

Wasps

Queens begin building new nests under eaves and in block walls

Why They're Active

St. George hits 70-degree days while northern Utah is still frozen, so desert pests get a months-long head start. Warming soil triggers ant colonies to expand and forage. Scorpions and spiders resume nightly hunting as overnight lows rise. Wasp queens seek sheltered nest sites on sun-warmed walls.

Our Spring Treatment Approach

  • Perimeter barrier treatments to stop ants before they enter
  • Foundation and block-wall treatments targeting scorpions and spiders
  • Early wasp nest removal before colonies grow large
  • De-webbing of exterior surfaces
  • Granular treatments for yard-nesting ants

Spring Prevention Tips:

  • Seal cracks in your foundation and block walls before pests find them
  • Clear yard debris and rock piles where scorpions shelter
  • Trim vegetation touching exterior walls
  • Check weatherstripping on doors — scorpions enter through gaps
  • Start protection early — desert pest season is already underway by March

☀ Summer Pest Control (June - September)

Summer is peak pest season in Washington County — and it's intense. Triple-digit heat drives scorpions and roaches indoors looking for water and shade, and the July-September monsoon triggers sudden spikes in ant and roach activity after every storm.

Common Summer Pests

Scorpions

Peak season — hunting at night and slipping indoors to escape the heat

Cockroaches

Turkestan and American roaches surge, especially after monsoon storms

Ants

Maximum foraging activity, with post-storm swarms during monsoon season

Wasps & Mosquitoes

Wasp colonies peak; mosquitoes breed in monsoon runoff and standing water

Why They're Active

Extreme heat accelerates insect metabolism and reproduction, but it also pushes desert pests toward the one reliable source of water and cool air around: your home. Irrigated yards are an oasis in the desert, concentrating pest activity around houses. Monsoon storms flush roaches out of sewers and trigger ant mating flights.

Our Summer Treatment Approach

  • Scorpion-focused treatments of block walls, foundations, and entry points
  • Roach treatments targeting drains, meter boxes, and moisture sources
  • Aggressive ant colony elimination treatments
  • Wasp and hornet nest removal services
  • Exterior barrier applications with heat-stable residual products

Summer Prevention Tips:

  • Fix outdoor leaks and drips — water attracts everything in the desert
  • Eliminate standing water after monsoon storms
  • Repair window and door screens, and add door sweeps
  • Shake out shoes, towels, and pool toys left outside — scorpions hide in them
  • Don't leave pet food or water dishes outside overnight

🍂 Fall Pest Control (October - November)

Fall in St. George is short and mild — daytime highs stay comfortable well into November. But as nights finally cool, scorpions, spiders, and rodents all start moving toward the warmth of your home. Fall exclusion work now prevents winter houseguests.

Common Fall Pests

Rodents

Mice and roof rats seeking indoor shelter as nights cool

Scorpions

Slipping indoors through gaps as overnight temperatures drop

Spiders

Black widows still active; house spiders move indoors for prey

Cockroaches

Moving from yards and sewers into warm wall voids and garages

Why They're Active

Cooling nights trigger survival instincts even in a mild climate. Roof rats move into attics and palm trees, mice follow utility lines into garages, and scorpions squeeze through gaps as small as a credit card's width seeking retained heat in block walls and slab foundations.

Our Fall Treatment Approach

  • Rodent exclusion services to seal entry points
  • Attic inspections for roof rat activity
  • Scorpion barrier treatments around foundations and entries
  • Spider control inside and outside
  • Garage and storage-area treatments

Fall Prevention Tips:

  • Seal gaps around pipes, wires, and utilities
  • Install door sweeps on all exterior doors
  • Screen attic and foundation vents
  • Pick up fallen fruit — it feeds roof rats and ants
  • Trim palm trees and shrubs away from the roofline

❄ Winter Pest Control (December - January)

St. George winters are mild — sunny 50-degree days are normal — so pests here never get the hard freeze that knocks them down up north. Rodents are the top concern, but roaches, black widows, and even ants stay active in and around the warmth of your home.

Common Winter Pests

Mice & Roof Rats

Active year-round, breeding in walls, attics, and garages

Cockroaches

Sheltering in warm wall voids, water heater closets, and drains

Black Widows

Holed up in garages, sheds, and storage boxes

Ants

Slow down but keep foraging on warm winter days

Why They're Active

Without sustained freezing temperatures, desert pests slow down rather than die off. Mice and rats reproduce every 3 weeks year-round, meaning a small winter problem becomes a major spring infestation. Warm spells bring ants and spiders right back out — which is why St. George homes need year-round protection, not seasonal treatments.

Our Winter Treatment Approach

  • Rodent trapping and removal programs
  • Exclusion repairs to prevent future entry
  • Monitoring stations to detect activity early
  • Garage, shed, and storage-area spider treatments
  • Attic inspections for roof rat activity

Winter Prevention Tips:

  • Store food in sealed, rodent-proof containers
  • Keep storage areas clean and organized
  • Don't leave garage doors open
  • Listen for scratching sounds in walls and attics at night
  • Wear gloves when digging through stored boxes — black widows winter there
